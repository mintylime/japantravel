Sensei's Survival Guide

Learning a language is learning a culture. Use these tips to blend in, make friends, and eat exceptionally well.

🤤 The Magic of "Delicious"

These two words are the simplest, most effective tools you have for connecting with locals over food. Use them enthusiastically!

美味しい

Oishii (oh-ee-shee)

The Universal Standard. Polite and acceptable anywhere, from a street vendor to a high-end Michelin restaurant.

旨い

Umai (oo-my)

Casual & Punchy. Think "Yum!" or "Man, this is good!". High-energy, widely used in casual stalls, and carries a friendly vibe that locals respond warmly to.

🚶 Solo Travel Tactics: Eateries

Navigating the city solo means you have unique opportunities to naturally blend in and make friends.

1

Target Tachinomi (Standing Bars)

These small, standing-only pubs are scattered throughout cities (especially around stations or in Osaka's Namba/Shinsekai). Tailor-made for solo patrons. You stand at a counter, order small plates, and naturally end up rubbing shoulders with locals.

2

Always Opt for the Counter (Kaunta)

Whether at a ramen shop, yakitori joint, or okonomiyaki grill, choose counter seating. Watching the chef cook gives you a front-row view, and throwing out an enthusiastic "Umai!" after your first bite is an instant icebreaker.

🍵 Golden Dining Etiquette

いただきます Itadakimasu (I humbly receive)
ごちそうさまでした Gochisousama deshita (Thank you for the meal)
  • • Chopsticks (Ohashi): Never stick them vertically into your rice (it resembles a funeral ritual). Always rest them horizontally on a chopstick rest or across your bowl.
  • • Passing Food: Never pass food from chopstick to chopstick. Place it on a small plate instead.
  • • Oshibori (Wet Towel): Use it to clean your hands before eating, but strictly avoid wiping your face or neck with it.
  • • Slurping is Good! Slurping noodles (like ramen or soba) is entirely acceptable. It cools the noodles down and compliments the chef!
