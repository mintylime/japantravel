Sensei's Guide: Culture & Etiquette

As your teacher, let me share a secret: polite manners open doors in Japan. Dining etiquette is highly regarded and a wonderful way to show respect. Master these golden rules and essential phrases to show appreciation like a local!

いただきます

Itadakimasu

"I humbly receive." Say this with hands pressed together right before you start eating to show gratitude for the food and to the people who prepared it.

ごちそうさまでした

Gochisousama deshita

"That was a feast." Say this after finishing your meal, or directly to the restaurant staff when leaving, to express immense appreciation.

Golden Dining Rules

  • • Chopsticks (Ohashi): Never stick them vertically into your rice (it resembles a funeral ritual). Always rest them horizontally on a chopstick rest (hashioki) or across your bowl.
  • • Passing Food: Never pass food from your chopsticks directly to someone else's chopsticks. Place it on a small plate instead.
  • • Oshibori (Wet Towel): You will be handed this at most restaurants. Use it to clean your hands before eating, but strictly avoid wiping your face or neck with it.
  • • Slurping is Good! Slurping noodles (like ramen or soba) is entirely acceptable. It helps cool the noodles down and compliments the chef!
